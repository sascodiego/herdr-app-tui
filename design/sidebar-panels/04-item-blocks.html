04 — Item blocks and overflow

One answer to both complaints: the two rows read as one item because they share a continuous band, and a tab reads as a child because its band starts two cells to the right of its parent — hierarchy is a step, not a drawn line. Overflow is never a mystery: long details truncate with `…`, and the list clips only between whole blocks with explicit counts above and below.

Tradeoff: the band and the guide spend one column and one visual rule on structure, but the item boundary and the parent/child link stop being a guess. Scroll indicators cost one row at each clipped edge.

Fixes: P1 the two rows share one `panel` band, so they read as one item; the selected band turns `surface` and keeps the bold name; P2 a child band starts two cells to the right of its parent, so indentation alone states the link — no connector lines and no accent on hierarchy; P3 horizontal overflow truncates with `…`: a path keeps its tail, a branch keeps its head, and an agent line keeps state and app before pane count; P4 vertical overflow clips only between blocks, with `↑ N more` / `↓ N more` on `dim`; a tap or key moves a whole block and keeps the selected block fully visible with one block of context; P5 keyboard focus keeps its own accent outline, separate from the selected band.

Data source: counts and attention come from the core; `state · app` comes from OSC 7501. No placeholder fields.
